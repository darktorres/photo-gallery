Abstract:

At present, most of the perceptual hash methods for image copyright protection rely on manually designed feature extraction and mapping, whose detection accuracy is insufficient. Some schemes based on deep learning are designed to consider a limited variety of content retention operations, which are not enough to deal with the increasingly severe situation of image copyright protection. In response to this situation, a novel Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based perceptual image hashing scheme is introduced in this paper. In this scheme, the training images are classified according to their original images and a Hadamard matrix is used to generate a hash center for each class in Hamming space. Then the Convolution Neural Network learns the feature extraction process of the image automatically, constrained by central quantization and distinct quantization, so that the hash code of each image converges to the hash center of their class, and generates the final hash sequence. The proposed scheme can successfully strike a balance between perceived robustness and discrimination capacity. Based on the test results on large-scale test sets, F1 scores, equal error rate (EER) and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves demonstrate the superiority of our scheme compared with some state-of-the-art schemes.

\n SECTION I.

Introduction

With the rapid development of personal mobile devices and mobile internet, photographers can conveniently upload and share their photos on the internet. However, this greatly increases the risk of images embezzlement. In addition, due to the popularity of various image-editing applications, it is increasingly common to tamper with the stolen images before upload them to circumvent the supervision of copyright. This has brought great difficulty to the image copyright registration and the detection of the suspected infringing images. In the past decades, people have relied on various types of digital watermarking [1], [2] to mark protected images and detect suspected infringing images. However, the embedding of digital watermarking is usually accompanied by some degree of image quality degradation, and an image that has been embezzled before the watermark was embedded could not be detected. In recent years, image oriented perceptual hashing technique has gradually attracted the attention of researchers. Perceptual image hashing can be regarded as a one-way mapping, which maps images to short sequences of a certain length. Perceptual image hashing has important application value in image copyright protection fields such as copy detection, image retrieval, copyright registration, etc.

Some copyright protection schemes [3], [4] use cryptographic hash functions such as SHA-1, SHA-256 and MD5 to generate image hash codes. However, the cryptographic hash functions are extremely sensitive to the modification of files [5]. Minor changes in files may lead to the generation of two entirely different hash codes, which is greatly limited in the field of copyright protection. A qualified perceptual hash scheme should satisfy two basic properties: (1) Perceptual robustness: the scheme should be robust to the deterioration in the transmit (e.g., size scaling, noise, watermark) and content-preserving operations (e.g., cropping, rotation, stretching, color temperature and tone change). That is, the hash codes of the images after these transformations should be similar to that of the original image; (2) Discrimination: there should be sufficient distance between the hash codes generated by two visually different images. The improvement of perceptual robustness and discrimination usually has a contradiction, so an ideal image perceptual hash method should maintain a proper balance between robustness and discrimination.

Conventional image perception hash schemes normally use manually designed methods to extract image features, which cannot represent the content of the perceived image adequately. In practical application, it is often necessary to use a variety of schemes in combination. The development of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) has brought new possibilities for image hashing technology. With the help of the feature learning ability of deep neural network, the effect of feature extraction can be effectively improved. CNN has been widely used in the field of image retrieval hashing. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is few research on generating perceptual image hashes using deep neural networks, and there are many shortcomings, such as the large number of original images required for training and the insufficient variety of transformations that can be resisted. In this work, we propose a new CNN-based perceptual image hashing scheme. The primary contributions of the proposed scheme are listed as follows:

  1. We greatly reduce the number of original images required for training. Our image hashing scheme does not divide the images into the identical and distinct then load them into the network in pairs at a proportion for training. Instead, we regard the images generated by the transformation of each original image as one category, and each category forms distinct pairs with the rest of the categories. Therefore, our scheme only uses 1,000 original images for training, and achieves a higher accuracy than some schemes using 200,000 original images for training.

  2. Our scheme can efficiently generate high-quality hash functions. By introducing the concept of hash center, and with the help of central quantization and distinct quantization, the hash code distances between the identical image pairs and distinct image pairs is constrained. The relationship between images is used from a global perspective rather than just a pair perspective.

  3. We built an image dataset specially used for image copyright protection. The dataset has the characteristics of high quality and high resolution, which can provide sufficient resolution and detail for various transformation operations common in the process of image infringement, and provide some images after malicious operations and degradation common in infringement for training and testing.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews the development and the current research status of perceptual image hashing; Section III introduces proposed scheme in detail, including the overall scheme architecture, training set generation method, hash center and model structure; Section IV gives the experimental setup and the comparison of the results; Section V draws the conclusion of this paper.

\n SECTION II.

Related Works

There have been many studies on image hashing schemes for copy detection. The common process is to extract image features first, then learn from the extracted features to generate hash sequences. Early methods rely on manually designed feature extraction steps. According to the types of extracted features, they can be divided into: hash based on transform coefficients and hash based on image visual features.

The transform coefficient based hash schemes transform the image data from spatial domain to frequency domain, then extract appropriate eigenvalues to encode and compress into perceptual hash sequences. Kim [6] proposed an image copy detection schemes by dividing the image into blocks and combining discrete cosine transform (DCT). Test images are reduced to a $8\\times 8$\n sub-image by intensity averaging, and the AC coefficients are used to generate hash sequence. Fridrich et al. replaced the DCT modes with the low-frequency DC-free random smoothing patterns generated from a secret key, and then quantized it through the threshold to generate a specific length of image hash sequence [7]. Fourier transform (DFT) is also applied to image hashing method. In [8], robust features are extracted from secondary images after DFT transformation. Patil and Sarode applied the center symmetric local binary pattern (CSLBP) to the LL band of Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to achieve compact length hashing [9]. Ou and Rhee proposed a simple key-based secure image hashing scheme combining 1-D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Radon transform [10]. Tang et al. adopted DCT to extract a stable characteristic matrix, then reduced data of feature matrix with local linear embedding (LLE) [11]. Liu and Huang randomly divided the preprocessed image into multiple overlapping blocks, obtained two characteristic matrices through invariant moments and low-frequency discrete cosine transform coefficients, and quantized the invariant distance between vectors in the characteristic matrix to form a compact hash code [12].

Methods based on image visual features usually rely on image features such as SIFT [13], HOG [14], GIST [15], LBP [16], HSV [17], etc. The image hashing method designed by Schneider and Chang using histogram features is the earliest research in this field [18]. Tang et al. are committed to solving the problem of rotation attacks. In 2013 [19], they proposed to use ring based entropy to build image hashes to resist rotation attacks. In 2015 [20], they further proposed a robust image hashing with ring partition and invariant vector distances, which can resist image rotation with any angle. In 2017 [21], they used the rotation invariant feature matrix, An image hashing scheme based on multidimensional scaling (MDS) is proposed. In 2019 [22], they designed a novel image hash by constructing a stable third-order tensor from the normalized image and then decomposing the third-order tensor using a tensor decomposition algorithm known as Tuck decomposition. In 2021 [23], They used frequency Tuned (FT) method to determine saliency map for hash operation, and applied Isometric Mapping (isomap) to the field of image hashing for the first time. In [24], Chuan used the combination of local texture and color angle features to generate image hash. Huang and Liu extracted the maximum gradient magnitude and corresponding orientation information from the RGB color channels. They further obtained Normalized histogram and local frequency coefficient features from the maximum gradient magnitude, which improved the processing ability of screen content images (SCIs) [25]. Su et al. Combined distance metric learning technology and supervised quantization strategy into the image authentication process, and proposed a personalized authentication framework [26]. Liang et al. found translation invariance, a new property of 2D-2D (bidirectional two-dimensional) PCA (principal component analysis), then designed an efficient hashing method for image copy detection combined with 2D-2D PCA and PCT (Polar Coordinate Transformation) [27].

The accuracy and the generation efficiency of hash sequence of the above methods depend on the manually designed image feature extraction algorithm and feature mapping function. These methods are not powerful enough to deal with complex semantic information in the face of the current massive image data, so they can not well deal with the complex and changeable image transformation forms. In recent years, the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) has developed rapidly. Compared with the manually designed feature extraction methods, CNN can obtain more internal features of images, and has been widely used in target detection, image classification and other fields. Since the CNNH [28] method was proposed in 2014, more and more image hash related research began to turn to Convolutional Neural Network. The representative ones are DSH [29], DPSH [30], HashNet [31], ADSH [32] etc.

However, these methods are Retrieval Oriented hash methods, whose goal is to search and return images with similar picture style or content from databases with a large number of images. Some researchers call them retrieval hash (The opposite is Perceptual Hashing). The method based on deep learning and oriented to copy detection focused in this paper did not appear until 2019. Li et al. proposed a perceptual hash method based on deep learning [33]. This method attempts to construct a group of codecs to restore the processed image. After training, the decoder is discarded and the output of the encoder is used as the feature of the image to generate the final hash. Due to the limited capacity of a single encoder, the author combines multiple codecs to cope with large-scale transformation and combined transformation. This method [33] takes the lead in applying deep learning to the field of Perceptual Hashing, but it is not an end-to-end method and the combination of multiple codecs are still manually designed. Qin et al. proposed a perceptual image hash content authentication method based on multi constraint convolution neural network [34]. This method uses CNN to extract image features from input images and then splice two full connection layers to generate the final perceptual hash sequence. Since the design goal of this method is to authenticate images, it mainly considers the images deterioration in the network transmission process, without considering the malicious editing of pirates. Liu Qin et al. proposed a deep hash image copy detection algorithm called DHCD [35]. This method obtains the salient features of image pairs by constructing a pair of multi-scale twin convolutional neural networks and combining the spatial pyramid hierarchical pooling method. For the extracted features, the final perceptual hash code is generated through the hash layer composed of full connection layers. Compared with the method of Qin et al. [34], it has increased the ability to deal with common attacks of image pirate such as cropping, large-scale rotation and watermarking. Experiments show that this scheme has significant advantages over the previous methods in terms of speed and overall accuracy, but the types of transformations it can deal with are still not rich enough, and composite transformations are not considered. In addition, the number of original images required for the training of the above two methods are more than 200 thousand.

The depth perceptual hash algorithm has the self-learning ability of convolutional neural network. Compared with the manually designed feature extraction process, it can obtain deeper image features. By constraining the output to the specified length and range to form a hash sequence, it can effectively improve the computational efficiency and reduce the pressure on storage when filtering and comparing in a large database. In this paper, we propose a novel perceptual image hash method based on CNN. The idea of classification greatly reduces the number of original images required for training. The introduction of the hash center method makes the same perceptual images close to their respective hash centers, and takes the relationship between all training images from a global perspective into consideration rather than just a pairwise perspective. Central quantization and distinct quantization constrain the perception of the identical image pair and distinct image pair respectively. Experimental results show that our method can meet the needs of image copyright protection and show better performance than many existing schemes.

\n SECTION III.

Method

The aim of the proposed scheme is to learn the function from the training set $\\chi =\\lbrace x_{i} \\rbrace ^{N}_{i=1}$\n composed of $N$\n images $x$\n in a supervised manner in $n$\n transformed images generated from $m$\n original image. Where image $x_{i}\\in \\mathbb {R}^{D}$\n. Let ${f}:x\\mapsto h \\in \\lbrace 0,1 \\rbrace ^{K}$\n denote the nonlinear hash function from the input space $\\mathbb {R}^{D}$\n to K-bit Hamming space $\\lbrace 0,1 \\rbrace ^{K}$\n. The hash function we designed can generate the corresponding hash code $h$\n for each image $x$\n. For variant images generated from the same original image, the generated hash sequence is close in Hamming space, while for variant images generated from different original images, the generated hash sequence is far away in Hamming space.

We achieve this learning goal through the idea of classification and the method of hash center. The overall pipeline is shown in Fig. 1. Different from the previous method, which only divides images into the identical and distinct categories and learns in pairs, we regard the images generated from the same original image as one category. That is, $\\chi =\\lbrace \\lbrace x_{i} \\rbrace ^{N} _{i=1},L\\rbrace $\n, where $L$\n represents the class label set of the training set $\\chi $\n, and the variant images generated from the same original image have the same class label. The specific transformation method and proportion composition will be introduced in detail in Section III-A; A set of pre-generated points $C=\\lbrace c_{1},c_{2},\\ldots,c_{m} \\rbrace \\subset \\lbrace 0,1 \\rbrace ^{K}$\n serve as the hash centers. There are sufficient distances between these points in Hamming space. The concept and generation method of hash centers are described in detail in Section III-B; The training set is input into the Convolutional Neural Network to extract features and learn, then the features are mapped to the hash code of the specified length through the hash layer spliced after CNN. See Section III-C for the specific network model; The central similarity quantization and distinct quantization are used to supervise the learning process of hash function which encourages the hash sequences generated by images of the same class to be close to the same public hash center, while the hash sequences generated by images of different classes are distributed in different hash centers. By calculating the distance between hash sequences, we can distinguish whether the image is a variant of another image, and can filter out suspected infringing images from a group of images.\n

A. Generation of Training Set

The difficulty of generating training sets from original image datasets is the selection of the maximum amplitude and gradient of various transformations, as well as the proportion of the number of different transformations. Previous studies generally include transformations such as cropping, rotation, scaling, compression, blur, noise, etc. In general, these studies consider few kinds of transformations and lack composite transformations. The main consideration is the loss of image quality in the process of image transmission and distribution. Insufficient consideration is given to the case that the pirates maliciously tamper with the original pictures to avoid copyright supervision in the process of infringement.

Through the investigation and experiment of image infringement cases on the Internet, the transformation method we proposed includes cropping, rotation, color temperature, tone change, gamma correction, scaling, watermarking, Gaussian noise, salt and pepper noise, Gaussian blur and other attacks. The cropping operation is carried out at random positions under different sizes of cropping windows. The rotation transformation includes clockwise and counterclockwise rotation. After the rotation, the black edges around the picture are removed and only the rectangular part in the center of the picture is intercepted. Gamma correction is used to simulate the brightness change during photo retouching. The watermark contains text and picture watermarks of different sizes and colors. We regard cropping, noise, blur and watermark as degradation in the transmission process and randomly add them to malicious attacks. This serves two purposes: It is as close as possible to the actual infringement cases; It helps to improve the resistance to the composite transformation. B. Hash Center

The concept of hash center was first proposed by Yuan et al [36]. The hash center is defined as a group of points $\\mathcal {C}=\\lbrace c_{i} \\rbrace ^{m} _{i=1} \\subset \\lbrace 0,1 \\rbrace ^{K}$\n in the K-dimensional Hamming space. Where $m$\n is the number of hash centers. The Hamming distance $D_{H}$\n between the two points must meet the following requirements:\\begin{equation*} \\frac { 1 }{ \\mathcal {N} }\\sum _{i=1}^{m} {D_{H}(c_{i},c_{j})}\\geq \\frac { K }{ 2 }\\qquad,\\tag{1}\\end{equation*}\nView Source\\begin{equation*} \\frac { 1 }{ \\mathcal {N} }\\sum _{i=1}^{m} {D_{H}(c_{i},c_{j})}\\geq \\frac { K }{ 2 }\\qquad,\\tag{1}\\end{equation*}\n where $\\mathcal {N}$\n is the number of combinations of different $c_{i}$\n and $c_{j}$\n, $(c_{i},c_{j})\\in \\mathcal {C}$\n.

From the above definition, we can see that the generation method of hash center is not limited. In fact, there are several methods to generate effective hash centers. Since our training set is of single label type, here we use the Hadamard matrix to generate the required hash centers. Hadamard matrix is an orthogonal square matrix composed of +1 and −1 elements. A $K \\times K$\n Hadamard matrix $H_{K}=[h_{a}^{1};\\ldots;h_{a}^{K}]$\n has the following properties:

1) Vector Inner Product is Zero

The inner product of any two lines of vectors $\\langle h_{a}^{i},h_{a}^{j} \\rangle = 0$\n. That is, the Hamming distance between any two lines of vectors $D_{H}(h_{a}^{i},h_{a}^{j})=\\frac { 1 }{ 2 }(K-\\langle h_{a}^{i},h_{a}^{j} \\rangle)=\\frac { K }{ 2 }$\n. So these vectors meet the requirements of serving as hash centers.

2) Size of the Matrix

The size $K$\n of the square array must be a power of 2 ($K=2^{n}$\n). The hash sequence bits such as 24 and 48 cannot be generated, but the hash sequence bits such as 16, 32 and 64 can be generated, which is sufficient to meet the use requirements.

3) Entries in the Matrix

All entries in the matrix are −1 or +1. The requirements of $\\mathcal {C} \\subset \\lbrace 0,1 \\rbrace ^{K}$\n can be met by replacing -1 with 0. In the specific operation, firstly, we refer to the method in [37] to generate a $K \\times K$\n Hadamard matrix $H_{K}$\n:\\begin{align*} H_{K} = \\left [{ {\\begin{array}{cc} H_{2^{n-1}} & H_{2^{n-1}} \\\\ H_{2^{n-1}} & -H_{2^{n-1}} \\\\ \\end{array} } }\\right]=H_{2} \\otimes H_{2^{n-1}},\\tag{2}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*} H_{K} = \\left [{ {\\begin{array}{cc} H_{2^{n-1}} & H_{2^{n-1}} \\\\ H_{2^{n-1}} & -H_{2^{n-1}} \\\\ \\end{array} } }\\right]=H_{2} \\otimes H_{2^{n-1}},\\tag{2}\\end{align*}\n where $K=2^{n}$\n, $H_{1}=\\left [{ {\\begin{array}{c} 1 \\end{array} } }\\right]$\n, $\\begin{aligned} H_{2}=\\left [{ {\\begin{array}{cc} 1 & 1 \\\\ 1 & -1 \\\\ \\end{array} } }\\right] \\end{aligned}$\n and $\\otimes $\n represents the Hadamard product. When the number of centers $m < K$\n, we select each row and replace −1 with 0 to form the hash centers. When $K\\le m\\le 2K$\n, We combine the two matrices to form $H_{2K}=[H_{K}, -H_{K}]^{T}$\n and generate hash centers from it.

C. Central and Distinct Quantification

We assign the pre-generated hash center $\\mathcal {C}=\\lbrace c_{1},c_{2},\\cdots,c_{m} \\rbrace $\n to the $m$\n classes of the generated training set $\\chi $\n. Then we assign the corresponding semantic hash center $\\mathcal {C'}=\\lbrace c'_{1},c'_{2},\\cdots,c'_{N} \\rbrace $\n to the data sample $x_{i}$\n in the training set $\\chi $\n. We refer to the method in [36] to maximize the likelihood probability to obtain the log maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate of the hash sequence $\\mathcal {H}=[h_{1},\\cdots,h_{N}]$\n:\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} \\mathrm {log}P(\\mathcal {H,C'}) \\varpropto \\mathrm {log}P(\\mathcal {C',H})P(\\mathcal {H}) \\\\&= \\sum _{i}^{N} {\\mathrm {log}P(c'_{i},h_{i})P(h_{i})}, \\tag{3}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} \\mathrm {log}P(\\mathcal {H,C'}) \\varpropto \\mathrm {log}P(\\mathcal {C',H})P(\\mathcal {H}) \\\\&= \\sum _{i}^{N} {\\mathrm {log}P(c'_{i},h_{i})P(h_{i})}, \\tag{3}\\end{align*}\n where $P(\\mathcal {C',H})$\n is the likelihood function, $p(\\mathcal {H})$\n is the prior distribution of hash codes and $P(c'_{i},h_{i})$\n is the conditional probability of the center $c'_{i}$\n of a given hash code $h_{i}$\n. $P(\\mathcal {C',H})$\n can be modeled as Gibbs distribution: $P(c'_{i},h_{i}) = \\frac { 1 }{\\alpha }\\mathrm {exp}(-\\beta D_{H}(c'_{i},h_{i}))$\n, Where $D_{H}$\n measures the Hamming distance between the hash code and its hash center, $\\alpha $\n and $\\beta $\n are constants.

The hash centers are binary vectors, so Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) can be used to measure the Hamming distance between the hash codes and there hash centers, $D_{H}(c'_{i},h_{i})=\\mathrm {BCE}(c'_{i},h_{i})$\n. Therefore, the conditional probability can be calculated by the following formula:\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} \\mathrm {log}P(c'_{i},h_{i}) \\varpropto - \\frac { 1 }{ K } \\sum {_{k\\in K}}(c'_{i,k} \\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c'_{i,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})), \\tag{4}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} \\mathrm {log}P(c'_{i},h_{i}) \\varpropto - \\frac { 1 }{ K } \\sum {_{k\\in K}}(c'_{i,k} \\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c'_{i,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})), \\tag{4}\\end{align*}\n We can see from the formula that as the conditional probability $P(c'_{i},h_{i})$\n increases, the Hamming distance between the hash code $h$\n and the corresponding hash center $c$\n is reduced, and vice versa. We bring formula 4 into equation 3 to get the center loss $L_{C}$\n optimization objectives:\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} L_{C}=\\frac { 1 }{ KN }\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k\\in K}[c'_{i,k}\\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c'_{i,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})],\\tag{5}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} L_{C}=\\frac { 1 }{ KN }\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k\\in K}[c'_{i,k}\\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c'_{i,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})],\\tag{5}\\end{align*}\n

$L_{C}$\n considers the relationship between the hash codes and their hash centers. We add another component $L_{D}$\n to constrain the relationship between each picture’s hash code and unrelated hash centers:\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} L_{D}=\\frac { 1 }{ KN(m-1) }\\sum _{j}^{m-1}\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k\\in K}[c_{j,k}\\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c_{j,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})],\\tag{6}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*}&\\hspace {-.5pc} L_{D}=\\frac { 1 }{ KN(m-1) }\\sum _{j}^{m-1}\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k\\in K}[c_{j,k}\\mathrm {log}h_{i,k} \\\\&+\\,(1-c_{j,k})\\mathrm {log}(1-h_{i,k})],\\tag{6}\\end{align*}\n Where $i \\ne j$\n.

In order to refine the generated hash code, we refer to the method in DNH [38] to introduce a bi-modal Laplacian prior as the quantization loss for quantization: $L_{Q}=\\sum {^{m}_{i\\ne j}(\\mid \\mid \\mid 2h_{i}-\\mathbf 1\\mid -\\mathbf 1\\mid \\mid _{1})}$\n, where $\\mathbf 1 \\in \\mathbb {R}^{K}$\n is an all-one vector. Since it is difficult to calculate the derivative of the non-smooth function $L_{Q}$\n, we adopt the smooth function$\\mathrm {log cosh}$\n to replace it: $\\mid x \\mid \\approx \\mathrm {log cosh} x$\n. Therefore, the final quantitative loss $L_{Q}$\n can be expressed as:\\begin{equation*}L_{Q}=\\frac { 1 }{ N }\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k=1}^{K}(\\mathrm {log cosh}(\\mid 2h_{i,k}-1 \\mid)-1),\\tag{7}\\end{equation*}\nView Source\\begin{equation*}L_{Q}=\\frac { 1 }{ N }\\sum _{i}^{N}\\sum _{k=1}^{K}(\\mathrm {log cosh}(\\mid 2h_{i,k}-1 \\mid)-1),\\tag{7}\\end{equation*}\n

Now, we have the final optimization problem:\\begin{equation*}\\mathrm {min} L_{T}=L_{C} +\\lambda _{1}L_{Q}- \\lambda _{2}L_{D},\\tag{8}\\end{equation*}\nView Source\\begin{equation*}\\mathrm {min} L_{T}=L_{C} +\\lambda _{1}L_{Q}- \\lambda _{2}L_{D},\\tag{8}\\end{equation*}\n Where $\\lambda _{1},\\lambda _{2}$\n are the hyper-parameters.

D. Model Structure

We adopt ResNet50 [39] to extract image features. ResNet is the abbreviation of deep residual network. Generally speaking, with the deepening of the layers of Convolutional Neural Network, the feature extraction ability of the network will be enhanced. But at the same time, it will also bring problems such as gradient disappearance and degradation. ResNet solves this problem by introducing the residual structure as shown in the Fig. 2. ResNet adds a quick connection between blocks and allows you to skip blocks directly when necessary. In this way, over fitting is avoided and the number of parameters is reduced. ResNet50 is a 50-layer deep residual network, its complete structure is shown in the Fig. 3. After the convolution layer, a hash layer composed of three full connection layers and ReLU is used to map the extracted features to the hash code of the required bits.\n

Experiments

A. Dataset

To our best knowledge, there are few datasets specifically for image copyright protection. Previous studies have generally transformed and generated training sets on the basis of general image datasets such as ImageNet [40], NUS WIDE [41] and MS COCO [42]. These datasets have a large number of pictures and rich types, but the images resolution below $500\\times500$\n, which does not meet the needs of operations such as blurring, scaling and watermark. Based on this situation, we propose a high-resolution image dataset specifically for image copyright protection. About 70% of images in the dataset are provided by individual photographers with authorization, and 30% of images are obtained from the copyright free image library on the Internet. This dataset contains a variety of scenes and contents such as buildings, streets, natural scenery, portraits, animals, plants, etc. taken by photographers. The resolution of the original images is greater than 4,$000\\times3$\n,000, and the maximum resolution of these images can reach 12,$000\\times8$\n,000. All 3,000 pictures are exposed accurately with clear details, which can provide sufficient details for various transformations. Fig. 4 is part of the representative pictures in the dataset.\n

Then we randomly added some proportion of noise, watermark, blur and some proportion of random cropping after malicious operation. A total of 84,000 transformed images were generated. The Fig. 5 shows some of the transformed images, and all the images were converted to $256\\times256$\n by bilinear. Each original image and their transformed images are loaded into the network for training as the same class after $224\\times224$\n random cropping. C. Perceptual Robustness

For a qualified perceptual hash method, the distance between the perceptual hash code generated from an original image and the perceptual hash code generated from its transformed image should be small enough. In order to evaluate this ability, we downloaded five standard test images of boat, baboon, house, airplane and pepper from the USC-SIPI [43] dataset (Shown in Fig. 6), transformed the images using the transformation method shown in Table 2, and generated 2,100 transformed images in total (Operations such as cropping and watermarking generated multiple images at each scale). We generated perceptual hash codes for all images and calculated the distance $D$\n between the hash codes of each transformed image and its respective original image. The results are shown in the Fig. 7. We can find that for most operations, the distance remains below 10, and the greater the transformation amplitude, the greater the distance. For a few, the distance is greater than 10 but less than 14.06 (In our scheme, two images whose Hamming distance between hash codes is lower than 14.06 are regarded as the same image. The details will be described in Section IV-E). Only a few points in Gaussian filtering and tone transformation are greater than 14.06, so our method shows good perceptual robustness on standard test images.

D. Discrimination Capability

The discrimination capability of different images is another important evaluation index of a perceptual image hash algorithm. We extracted 800 different images from the NUS-WIDE [41] dataset, including streets, animals, plants, natural scenery, urban architecture, portraits and other subjects. We generated corresponding perceptual hash codes for each image, then calculate the distance $D$\n of perceptual hash codes between each two pairs of all 800 images, generating a total of $C_{800}^{2}=319, 600$\n distances. The standard deviation is 3.22, and the mean value of these distances is 20.21, which is obviously greater than the distance between images in Section IV-C, indicating that the perceptual hash codes generated by our scheme can effectively distinguish different perceptual images.

E. Content Identification Capability

We compared our scheme horizontally with some existing schemes. RP-IVD [20] is a conventional method based on image visual features. CNMC [34], IFNN [33], DSH [29] and DBDH [44] are based on deep learning. Where CNMC and IFNN are specially designed for picture copyright protection. DSH and DBDH are Retrieval Oriented hash schemes. In order to fairly conduct the comparisons, we made some adjustments to the training process for the two schemes. We took the same dataset as in Section IV-B and used the same data augmentation method to generate the training set. ResNet50 was also used as the feature extraction network, which is the same as our scheme. All schemes were compared on the premise of generating 64 bits hash code. Because the discrimination capability and perceptual robustness are contradictory, a single index can not fully reflect the performance of the method in copyright protection applications. So we take the comprehensive index combining the discrimination capability and perceptual robustness of methods as the horizontal comparison standard.

In this round of test, we randomly selected 1, 200 original images from the ImageNet [40] image dataset, transformed the images according to the transformation operations in Table 2 to generate a total of $1, 200\\times 200=240,000$\n test images, and calculated the distance $D$\n between the perceptual hash code of each original image and the perceptual hash code of the image generated through transformation. For each original image, we randomly selected 200 different images from the remaining images in the ImageNet image dataset each time, and calculated the distance between their perceptual hash codes.

Through the above steps, we have performed 480,000 rounds of detection for each scheme, and the ratio of the identical image pair to distinct image pair is 1:1. If the distance $D(H^{(1) },H^{(2) })$\n between two images is less than the given threshold $T$\n, the two images are determined to be the same image, when $D > T$\n, they are determined to be different images. When the selected threshold $T$\n is different, we can get two indicators: false acceptance rate (FAR) and false rejection rate (FRR). The false acceptance rate (FAR) represents the probability that the distance between the generated perceptual hash codes is less than or equal to the threshold $T$\n when a pair of images is a distinct pair. The false rejection rate (FRR) represents the probability that the distance between the generated perceptual hash codes is greater than the threshold $T$\n, when a pair of images is an identical pair. The specific calculation method is shown in the equation 9:\\begin{align*} \\mathrm {FAR}(T)=&\\mathrm {Pr}(D(\\mathrm {H}^{(1) },\\mathrm {H}^{(2) })\\le T \\mid \\mathrm {distinct \\; pair}), \\\\ \\mathrm {FRR}(T)=&\\mathrm {Pr}(D(\\mathrm {H}^{(1) },\\mathrm {H}^{(2) })> T \\mid \\mathrm {identical \\; pair}), \\tag{9}\\end{align*}\nView Source\\begin{align*} \\mathrm {FAR}(T)=&\\mathrm {Pr}(D(\\mathrm {H}^{(1) },\\mathrm {H}^{(2) })\\le T \\mid \\mathrm {distinct \\; pair}), \\\\ \\mathrm {FRR}(T)=&\\mathrm {Pr}(D(\\mathrm {H}^{(1) },\\mathrm {H}^{(2) })> T \\mid \\mathrm {identical \\; pair}), \\tag{9}\\end{align*}\n

From FAR and FRR, we can calculate two other indicators: $F_{1}$\n score and equal error rate (EER). The higher the $F_{1}$\n score, the smaller the ERR indicating that the identification performance of the perceptual hash method is better. The calculation method of $F_{1}$\n score is shown in equation 10:\\begin{equation*}F_{1}=\\mathop {\\mathrm {max}}\\limits _{T} \\left \\{{ \\frac {2 \\cdot [1-\\mathrm {FAR}(T)]\\cdot [1-\\mathrm {FRR}(T)] }{[1-\\mathrm {FAR}(T)]+[1-\\mathrm {FRR}(T)] } }\\right \\}, \\tag{10}\\end{equation*}\nView Source\\begin{equation*}F_{1}=\\mathop {\\mathrm {max}}\\limits _{T} \\left \\{{ \\frac {2 \\cdot [1-\\mathrm {FAR}(T)]\\cdot [1-\\mathrm {FRR}(T)] }{[1-\\mathrm {FAR}(T)]+[1-\\mathrm {FRR}(T)] } }\\right \\}, \\tag{10}\\end{equation*}\n

EER is the value that makes $\\mathrm {FRR}(T)=\\mathrm {FAR}(T)$\n under a certain value of $T$\n, under which, the two indicators (the discrimination capability and perceptual robustness) are relatively balanced. Table 4 lists the $F_{1}$\n scores and EER values of various schemes, and Table 6 lists the $F_{1}$\n scores of various schemes under different transformation operations. It can be seen that the $F_{1}$\n score of our scheme is extremely close to 1 under most transformations. Our scheme performs particularly well on Scaling and Rotation+Cropping, with only Gamma Correction having smaller results than that of DSH. In some transformations (such as SP Noise and Color Temperature), our scheme has only slight advantages over DSH or CNMC. Although the average $F_{1}$\n scores of RP-IVD and DBDH are smaller than that of other schemes, they still achieve $F_{1}$\n scores above 0.9 on Gamma Correction and Color Temperature.

The total consuming time of generating hash codes for 1,000 images was considered as the execution time, then the average execution time was calculated as the final result. The results are shown in Table 5. We can observe from Table 5 that the average execution time of our scheme is significantly faster than that of CNMC, IFNN and RP-IVD. Since our scheme, DSH and DBDH are all trained under 64 bits using ResNet50, so the generation speed is similar, and DSH is slightly faster than ours. This indicates that our scheme can achieve higher accuracy with faster or similar generation speed. Our solution has qualified performance in terms of time complexity.

H. Application in Copyright Protection

Retrieving of stolen images is a common application scenario in image copyright protection. In order to prove the application value of our scheme in image copyright protection, we designed the following comparative experiments to evaluate the ability of various methods in retrieving stolen images. In the practical retrieval application scenario, the threshold $T$\n is usually appropriately reduced to obtain a lower False Positive Ratio at the expense of some Recall Ratio. We randomly selected 100 images from the ImageNet dataset as test images. We randomly selected three kinds of transformation from Table 2 each time to form composite transformation to process images, and generated 100 stolen images for each test image, then dispersed them into 9,900 unrelated images. The distance $D$\n between the perceptual hash of these 10,000 images and the perceptual hash of the test image was calculated. The threshold $T$\n was dynamically adjusted to make the False Positive Ratio lower than 0.1 and 0.01, respectively. The Recall Ratio of all schemes were recorded. The average value of the test results of 100 test images was shown in Table 7. It can be observed that in this test scenario, our scheme has obvious advantages in Recall Ratio. When the False Positive Ratio is 0.01, more than 80% of stolen images can still be detected, which indicates that our method has some application value in stolen images detection.

\n SECTION V.

Conclusion

In this work, a novel perceptual image hashing scheme based on convolutional neural network (CNN) is proposed. We take the images generated by the transformation of the same original image as a same class, train them in the form of classification, and introduce the hash center method to consider the relationship between the training images from the global rather than just in pairs. Our scheme greatly reduces the number of original images required for training. We combine central quantization with distinct quantization to constrain the distance between the hash codes of the training images. Experimental results show that our scheme has good perceptual robustness and discrimination ability, and maintains a splendid balance between them. Compared with some existing excellent schemes, our scheme has advantages in $F_{1}$\n score, EER and ROC curve. In addition, our scheme required far fewer original images for training than other schemes (1,000 vs. more than 200,000). In view of the current situation that there are few datasets for image copyright protection and the image resolution is not high enough, we build a high-resolution dataset for image copyright protection, and provide some transformed images for perceptual image hashing training. This scheme has broad application prospects in image copyright registration and suspected infringing image retrieval.
